# Mazmatics marketing playbook

The redesigned site is live. The hard part — turning the site into a sales engine — starts now. This is a 90-day plan calibrated to a solo author working in spare time (1–3 hours per week, no paid budget).

---

## Where we are (May 2026)

- **Site:** redesigned, fast, SEO-equipped (per-page meta, JSON-LD Book + Person + Organization, sitemap, 5 redirects preserving link equity).
- **Audience:** primarily parents of kids 7–10 who need help liking maths. Top traffic source: word-of-mouth referrals seeking credibility. NZ/AU strongest, US/UK growing.
- **Distribution:** Amazon AU/US/UK. Locale-aware buy + review CTAs already shipped.
- **Channels:** `@mazmaticsfun4kids` on Instagram and `mazmaticsfunforkids` on Facebook — both dormant. MailerLite list with footer signup but no automated welcome sequence.
- **Press:** Stuff.co.nz / Dom Post coverage from book launch. Already surfaced on home (compact strip) and /about (lite-embed video).
- **Reviews:** unknown count. Need an Amazon review boost — see Week 2 below.

The single highest-leverage move available right now is **getting more Amazon reviews from existing customers**. Reviews compound on Amazon's algorithm and credibility for new visitors. Everything else (social, content, community) supports that loop.

---

## North-star metrics (track monthly)

1. **Amazon reviews** count and average. The headline number.
2. **Email list size** — new signups per month.
3. **Search Console organic clicks** — to see what's actually pulling traffic in.
4. **Social engagement** — saves and shares, not vanity follower count.

Everything else (impressions, page views, Likes) is secondary.

## 30/60/90-day plan

### Days 1–30: foundation + first activations

**Week 1 — instrumentation**
- [ ] Register **Google Search Console** for `mazmatics.com`. Submit `sitemap.xml`. (15 min — see `founder-actions.md`)
- [ ] Register **Bing Webmaster Tools** (10 min, can import from GSC)
- [ ] Validate the live site in **Facebook Sharing Debugger**, **Twitter Card Validator**, and **Google Rich Results Test** for `/`, `/about`, `/get-the-book`, `/free-sample`, `/write-a-review`. Force-refresh any caches.
- [ ] **Reactivate Instagram + Facebook** — single post on each: "Mazmatics has a new home. New site, same book, same scrappy maths joy. Take a look →" linked to home. This is the "we're back" pulse.

**Week 2 — first reviews push (highest leverage)**
- [ ] Email your existing MailerLite list with a single, warm ask: *"If Mazmatics was useful for your kid, would you leave a 1-2 sentence review on Amazon? Here's a direct link →"* Send to your matched-region list (AU folks → AU link). The redesigned `/write-a-review` page does the locale matching automatically — link parents there.
- [ ] Post on Instagram + Facebook: a kid drawing in the book + a quote from a parent → CTA to the new `/write-a-review` page.
- [ ] Personally email the 5–10 friends/family/parents who first championed the book. Direct ask. They're your most loyal evangelists.

**Week 3 — content cadence begins**
- [ ] Establish your posting rhythm. Sustainable target for a solo author: **2 posts per week** on Instagram. Facebook can mirror Instagram automatically (Meta's cross-posting).
- [ ] Pull a post from the **content pillars** below. Don't over-think — done > perfect.
- [ ] Add a footer to your weekly email signature: *"Mazmatics: Fun Math 4 Kids — mazmatics.com"*. Free, passive, hits everyone you correspond with.

**Week 4 — review what worked**
- [ ] Check Search Console: which pages got impressions? Which queries? (Won't be much yet — Google takes 2–4 weeks to crawl + report.)
- [ ] Check Amazon: any new reviews?
- [ ] Note what felt sustainable, what didn't. Prune what you hated doing.

### Days 31–60: expand + nurture

**Goals:** consistent posting, growing email list, first organic discovery.

- **Posting cadence:** 2 IG/FB posts per week + 1 mailing-list email per fortnight.
- **Email welcome sequence:** set up a 3-email automation in MailerLite for new signups (template in `post-ideas.md`).
- **Reply to every comment** in the first 60 days, however small. Algorithmic + community signal.
- **Reach out to 3 NZ/AU parenting accounts** (homeschool, Reggio, primary teachers) for a polite "hi, here's a free sample" email. No ask, just a gift. Some will become repeat sharers.
- **First teacher / school resource site outreach** — places like NZmaths.co.nz, AustralianTeachersOfMaths, BBC Bitesize blog (UK). Send a free PDF sample with a one-paragraph intro.

### Days 61–90: momentum

**Goals:** Search Console showing real organic traffic, mailing list >100 net new, first 5+ Amazon reviews from this push.

- **Volume 2 announcement teaser** — see `Vol. 2 momentum` section below. Start dropping breadcrumbs about the next book around day 60–70.
- **First user-generated content reshare** — find a parent or teacher post about Mazmatics in the wild and reshare with thanks. UGC compounds trust faster than your own posts.
- **One long-form post** — could be a Substack, a Medium, or just a static `/blog/why-i-wrote-mazmatics` page. Pick a story you haven't told publicly: the moment your kid said "I like maths"; the page you cut from Vol. 1; the math joke that sparked the book.

## Content pillars

Five buckets to rotate through. Variety beats forced themes — pick whatever's true and ready in the moment.

### 1. **The book in action** (35% of posts)
Photos of real kids using the book. Drawings, scribbles, half-finished pages. The unpolished mess is the point — it shows the book is a tool kids actually engage with, not a coffee-table prop.

### 2. **Behind the scenes** (25%)
The making-of. Sketches of Lindy. Pages you cut. Math jokes that didn't make it. Process shots of you drawing/writing. This builds the parasocial connection that turns followers into evangelists.

### 3. **Maths-joy moments** (20%)
Tiny aha moments — a 3-line story, a riddle, a number pattern, a kid quote about math. Standalone joy, no CTA. These get saved + shared.

### 4. **Parent-helpful** (15%)
"3 things to say when your kid says 'I'm bad at maths'." "Why we don't do flash cards at home." "What 'home play, not homework' actually looks like." Shareable parenting content that happens to be about maths.

### 5. **Amazon review push** (5%)
Subtle, infrequent. *"Reviews are how other parents find this book. If yours did, would you leave 1-2 lines? →"* — link to `/write-a-review`. **Hit this every 4–6 weeks max.** More than that and it reads as needy.

## Post-idea bank (specific, ready-to-write)

Pick one per posting day. Most of these need only a single photo + 2–4 sentences of caption.

### The book in action
1. **Multi-kid montage** — 4–6 photos of different kids drawing in the book, one image. Caption: "Six kids, six different ways to attack page 12. None of them look alike. That's the point."
2. **Before/after a single page** — kid started page blank, finished with their additions. "Same page, three minutes apart."
3. **A kid's wrong answer they kept** — celebrate the keeping, not the right answer. "Mistakes are landings, not failures. Page 47, before she figured it out — and after."
4. **Read-aloud evening** — book on the kitchen table, kids around. "Saturday night isn't always Netflix in this house."
5. **Bookmark snapshots** — page kids folded down to come back to. "Whatever they fold down is the page that worked. Page 19 is winning this week."

### Behind the scenes
6. **Cover-design alternates** — early drafts of the book cover before the final. "These are the covers Mazmatics nearly had."
7. **Lindy character study** — how Lindy evolved. Pencil sketch → final illustration.
8. **A page you cut** — an actual page that didn't make Vol. 1, with one line on why.
9. **Your own desk** — half-finished Vol. 2 pages, coffee, mess. Caption: "Vol. 2 looks like this right now."
10. **The math joke that started the book** — three sentences. "I told my kid 'why was 6 afraid of 7?' She didn't laugh. So I drew it."

### Maths-joy moments
11. **A pattern post** — three-frame puzzle. "Look at this for 10 seconds. What's the pattern?" Solve in caption or in the next post.
12. **Number-of-the-week** — 27, 36, 100. Three weird facts about the number. Kids will read it.
13. **A "bad" maths question kids actually like** — "Would you rather have $1 a day for a month, or 1 cent doubling every day for a month?" Solve next post.
14. **Maths jokes** — pun-level, low-stakes. "I have a maths joke about a paradox but I won't tell it. It's irrational."
15. **A kid said this** — a quote from your kid (or any kid) about maths that made you stop. "She said 'maths is just patterns having a conversation.' She's nine."

### Parent-helpful
16. **Home play vs homework** — short carousel: "What 'home play' looks like" / "What 'homework' looks like" / "Why one of them is the trap."
17. **The 'I'm bad at maths' moment** — 3 things to say (or NOT say) when your kid says this.
18. **Why screens aren't the math fix** — short, opinionated, links back to "pencil first" pillar of the book.
19. **The pencil-grip post** — "This is how my kid held her pencil before Mazmatics. This is now. Same kid, three months apart."
20. **What I tell people about maths education** — opinionated 3-line post about state-of-the-art primary-maths thinking. Earn the title "the dad who actually thinks about this."

### Reviews
21. **The review ask** (use sparingly) — one parent quote + the ask + the link. *"Karen left this review last week. If Mazmatics has been useful for your kid, would you leave one too? It's how other families find us. →"*

## Community engagement (the multiplier)

Strangers don't buy from strangers. The leverage isn't broadcasting; it's *participating*.

### Where to spend the time

- **Reply to every comment** on your own posts for the first 90 days. Even "thanks!" When someone takes 2 seconds to comment, you've earned a 30-second conversation.
- **Comment on 3 other accounts a week.** Not your own. Other parenting / homeschool / kids-books accounts. **Do not promote Mazmatics in those comments.** Just be a useful voice in their replies. After 4–6 weeks, those creators recognize you. Some will reciprocate organically.
- **Find one Slack/Discord/forum** where parents gather to discuss kids' learning. *Lurk for 2 weeks before posting.* Then become a useful community member. Plug Mazmatics only when directly relevant to a question someone asked. The "show up regularly, be useful, never be the loudest" pattern outperforms paid ads for this audience.

### Ideas that compound

- **The Mazmatics gallery** — a recurring monthly post: "this month's pages from real kids." Encourages sharing back. Tag every parent/kid (with permission).
- **Kid-illustrator features** — pick one kid each month who's drawn in the book. Permission from the parent, share their drawing on the grid with credit. This converts kids into mini-evangelists who tell their friends.
- **Free sample as a gift** — anytime someone asks "is your book good for X?", reply with the free sample link. No-pressure proof.
- **Birthday gift moments** — January birthday, July birthday. Position the book as a non-obvious gift. (Adults love non-obvious gifts.)

## Building momentum for Vol. 2

The single biggest mistake authors make on book #2 is launching it cold. The pre-launch is the launch.

### 90 days before Vol. 2 ships

- **Drop teaser pages** — a single illustration from Vol. 2 every other week. Not the cover, not the title — just art. Build curiosity.
- **Open a "Vol. 2 mailing list"** — separate signup, "be the first to know when Vol. 2 ships." Make it explicit. People love being first.
- **Behind-the-scenes** weekly — a process post. Sketch, ink, page-spread reveal. Vol. 2 isn't an announcement; it's a season of content.

### 30 days before launch
- **Cover reveal** — single post, pinned. Big moment.
- **Pre-order setup** — even if it's just a notify-me form, give people a way to commit early.
- **Email sequence #2** — "Vol. 2 is two weeks away. Here's what's in it." Three emails, three angles.

### Launch day
- **A single email** to the Vol. 2 list. *"It's here. Here's the link."* Don't dilute with multiple sends.
- **Single Instagram post + Facebook + a Story.** Pin the post. Reply to every comment.
- **Direct ask to existing Vol. 1 reviewers** — "you reviewed Vol. 1; if you'd like a free copy of Vol. 2 in exchange for a review, reply yes." This pre-seeds Vol. 2 reviews.

### 30 days after launch
- **First-week reviews push** — the same email that worked for Vol. 1, now for Vol. 2.
- **Creator outreach** — parenting accounts you've been engaging with for 90 days. Now they've seen you. Send them a copy.

The pattern: *every channel you build for Vol. 1 makes Vol. 2 launch easier, then Vol. 3 trivial.* The compounding is real but slow.

## What NOT to do

A few traps for indie authors. Avoiding these is easier than fixing them.

- **Don't run paid ads yet.** With <5 reviews and a still-growing email list, paid ads burn money on visitors who bounce. Earn the first 50 reviews organically; *then* paid ads have something to amplify.
- **Don't overpost.** Two posts a week, sustainable for years, beats six posts a week for two months and then nothing. The brand voice memo says "warm, playful, growth-mindset" — that's not a vibe you can fake under deadline pressure.
- **Don't chase virality.** One tweet trending doesn't sell books. Saved/shared educational content does, slowly.
- **Don't apologize for being small.** Parents BUY from small-author indie books once they trust the work. Don't lead with "I'm just a dad." Lead with the work.
- **Don't gate the free sample.** It's a 3-page PDF. Make it easier to download than to ask for. (We already did this — `/free-sample` lets them download without an email.)

## Measurement (monthly check-in, ~15 minutes)

Once a month, log into:

1. **Search Console** — clicks, impressions, top queries, top pages
2. **Google Analytics** — sessions, top pages, conversion events (`amazon_cta_click`, `free_sample_downloaded`, `mailing_list_submit`)
3. **MailerLite** — list growth, open rates
4. **Amazon Author Central** — review count, ranking, sales rank
5. **Instagram + Facebook insights** — saves and shares (NOT followers)

Write the numbers down somewhere persistent (a single Notion page works). Month-over-month trend matters more than any single number.

---

## When in doubt

The brand voice is *warm, playful, growth-mindset, scrappy*. Not polished, not corporate, not loud. Every post passes through the question: *"would my own kid be embarrassed if her teacher saw this?"* If yes, soften it.

The audience is *parents who want their kids to like maths*. They are not maths teachers, not curriculum experts, not children themselves. Speak to the parent.

If a marketing tactic feels icky, it probably is. Skip it. The book is good. The work is good. The slow path works.
